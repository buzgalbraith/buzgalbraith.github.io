I am a PhD student in Computer Science at the [Khoury College of Computer Sciences](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/), Northeastern University, where I work in the [Gyori Lab for Computational Biomedicine](https://gyorilab.github.io/) under the advisement of Prof. Benjamin M. Gyori. My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, databases, and biology: I build methods and systems that turn heterogeneous biomedical data into structured, connected, and statistically reliable knowledge.

Modern biomedical research generates data faster than we can make sense of it. The same concept is described a dozen different ways across studies, portals, and ontologies, and the tools we use to reason over that data are only as trustworthy as the links between its entities. Much of my work is about closing that gap for example by automatically constructing biomedical knowledge graphs, matching and grounding entities across ontologies, and quantifying the uncertainty in those links so downstream analyses can be trusted.

My research interests include:

* **Biomedical knowledge graph construction** — automatically extracting structured knowledge from data portals, metadata, and experimental datasets ([DGLink](/portfolio/dglink/)).
* **Ontology matching & entity grounding** — mapping plain text and equivalent classes across biomedical ontologies, including weakly supervised, embedding-based approaches ([cross-ontology mapping](/portfolio/cross-ontology-mapping/)).
* **Uncertainty quantification for entity linking** — using conformal inference to provide statistical risk control when linking entities to standard identifiers ([RCPS entity linking](/portfolio/rcps-entity-linking/)).
* **Computational genomics & distributed systems** — efficient, similarity-aware load balancing for distributed genomic indices ([genomic index load-balancing](/portfolio/genomic-load-balancing/)).

Before starting my PhD, I spent a year as a Machine Learning Engineer on the Advanced Computational Infrastructure Team at the [Kaiser Permanente Division of Research](https://divisionofresearch.kaiserpermanente.org/), where I containerized and deployed clinical breast-cancer risk models across hundreds of thousands of patient records and built NLP models for identifying cancer in pathology reports. I have also held research positions at the Dey Lab (Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center), the Hochwagen Lab (NYU Department of Biology), and the Cirrone Lab (NYU Langone Health).

I earned my M.S. in Data Science with a focus in Biomedical Informatics from New York University, where my thesis applied knowledge graphs to understanding cancer risk and treatment recommendations, and my B.A. in Data Science and Economic Theory (with a minor in Mathematics) from NYU.
